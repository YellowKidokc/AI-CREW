# PROMPT: The Killer Questions — Paper Destruction and Rebuild

tags: #prompt #paper #kill-questions #rewrite #strengthener

**Target:** Any AI, any paper, any stage
**Purpose:** Force rewrites that make the paper stronger, not cleaner

---

## How To Use

Run these questions against any paper, article, or chapter. Each question has a REWRITE TRIGGER — if the answer reveals a gap, the paper must be rewritten to close it. Not edited. Rewritten. The gap becomes load-bearing content.

Do not answer these questions FOR the author. Present the question and the gap. The author rewrites.

---

## TIER 1 — THE KILLS (if the paper survives these, it's real)

### Q1: What does the reader believe BEFORE this paper that they will not believe AFTER?

If the answer is "nothing changes" — the paper has no thesis. It's a tour, not an argument. A paper that doesn't change a belief is a paper that doesn't need to exist.

**Rewrite trigger:** State the before-belief and after-belief in one sentence each. If you can't, the paper doesn't know what it's doing. Rewrite until you can.

### Q2: What is the ONE thing the hostile reader will say, and where in the paper do you make them say it too early?

Every strong paper has a moment where the reader's objection is anticipated and then answered. If the objection is never raised, the reader raises it alone and puts the paper down. If it's raised too late, they already stopped trusting you.

**Rewrite trigger:** Find the strongest objection. Place it where the reader would think of it. Answer it there. If it's not in the paper, add it. If it's in the wrong place, move it.

### Q3: Delete the first two paragraphs. Does the paper get better?

Most papers start with throat-clearing. "In recent years..." "It has long been recognized..." "This paper seeks to..." The real paper starts where the author stops being polite and starts being dangerous.

**Rewrite trigger:** If the paper improves with the first two paragraphs deleted, delete them. Start where the energy starts.

### Q4: What claim in this paper would the author be AFRAID to say at a physics department seminar?

That claim is either the paper's strongest contribution or its weakest link. Either way, it's the center of gravity. If the paper buries it in the middle or hedges it with qualifiers, the paper is hiding from itself.

**Rewrite trigger:** Find the scary claim. Move it to the front. State it without hedging. Then defend it with everything the paper has.

### Q5: If this paper is RIGHT, what breaks?

Not "what's interesting." What breaks. What existing result, textbook, or consensus position is wrong if this paper is right? If nothing breaks, the paper is either trivial or lying about its implications.

**Rewrite trigger:** Name what breaks. Put it in the paper. If the paper doesn't have the courage to say what it breaks, it doesn't have the courage to be right.

---

## TIER 2 — THE STRUCTURE KILLS (order and flow)

### Q6: Can you draw the argument as a chain where each link depends on the previous one?

If yes, check: is there a link the reader won't grant? That's where you need more work. If no — if it's a web, not a chain — the paper will feel like "a bunch of good points" instead of "one argument that builds."

**Rewrite trigger:** Draw the chain. Find the weakest link. Strengthen it or restructure so it's not load-bearing.

### Q7: Does the paper earn the right to its conclusion, or does it assert it?

"Therefore X follows" only works if the reader has been walked through every step. If any step was skipped — "it can be shown that" / "it is clear that" / "obviously" — the reader didn't follow, they were told. Told isn't earned.

**Rewrite trigger:** Find every "it can be shown" / "clearly" / "obviously." Replace each with the actual showing. If you can't show it, downgrade the claim.

### Q8: Read the last paragraph first. Then the first. Is there an arc?

The first paragraph makes a promise. The last paragraph keeps it — or breaks it in a way that's better than keeping it. If the last paragraph is a generic summary ("In this paper we have shown..."), the paper ended before it finished.

**Rewrite trigger:** The last paragraph should say something the first paragraph made impossible to predict but inevitable in retrospect. If it doesn't, rewrite the ending.

---

## TIER 3 — THE THEOPHYSICS-SPECIFIC KILLS

### Q9: Is the science doing work, or is it decoration on theology?

If you can remove the equations and the theological claim still stands on its own, the equations are decoration. If you can remove the theology and the physics still stands on its own, the theology is decoration. In Theophysics, BOTH must be load-bearing or the paper is one-sided.

**Rewrite trigger:** Remove all equations. Does the theological argument collapse? Remove all theology. Does the physics argument collapse? If either survives alone, the bridge is fake. Rewrite until both sides need each other.

### Q10: Does the paper pass the Conciliatory Rule?

Does it hedge toward science at theology's expense? Does it hedge toward theology at science's expense? The framework holds both as dual projections. If the paper quietly collapses toward one, it has abandoned the mission.

**Rewrite trigger:** Find every sentence that hedges one direction. Restate it so both sides are held together. If you genuinely cannot, flag it and move on — do not force it.

### Q11: What would make the author abandon this claim?

If the answer is "nothing" — the claim isn't scientific, it's faith wearing a lab coat. If the answer is specific and testable — that kill condition belongs in the paper, explicitly, where the reader can see it.

**Rewrite trigger:** State the kill condition. Put it in the paper. If the kill condition is already in the paper, check: is it real? Would the author actually abandon the claim if it triggered? If not, find a real one.

### Q12: Is this the same thing said three times, or three things said once each?

Theophysics papers tend toward restating the same insight in physics language, then theology language, then plain language. That's the descent — and it's good. But if those three restatements don't each ADD something the others didn't, they're redundant, not complementary.

**Rewrite trigger:** For each restatement, identify what it adds that the others don't. If it adds nothing, cut it. The descent must carry new information at each level.

---

## HOW TO RUN THIS

Give the AI the paper and say:

> Run the Killer Questions against this paper. For each question, give me:
> 1. The question
> 2. Your honest answer
> 3. Whether the rewrite trigger fires
> 4. If it fires, what specifically needs to change
>
> Do not soften. Do not hedge. The point is to find what kills the paper so I can put it IN the paper.
