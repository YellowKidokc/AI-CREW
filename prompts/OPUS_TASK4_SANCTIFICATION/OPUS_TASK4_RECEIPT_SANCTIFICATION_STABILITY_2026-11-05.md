# Headline (after the addendum run)

**The whole "abide in me" question comes down to one premise: does maturity
built under Grace decay when Grace stops?**

- Decays (variant 2b): **100% fall** after Grace is withdrawn (median 52 time
  units later). This is the dispatch prediction, now derived from a named
  premise instead of assumed.
- Persists (variant 2a): **0.2% fall.** Grace can be withdrawn and the person
  stays at +1.

The canon (`GRACE_REFERENCE_DOCS`) already says Grace is what *builds*
maturity ("realigns the attractor basin"). It does not say whether maturity
*lasts* without Grace. That is the open question for David, and it is
answerable from Scripture and tradition, not from the model.

See "Addendum" at the end. The first-pass analysis below is kept as run.

# One-paragraph answer (first pass, before the reference docs)

The dispatch prediction ("without ongoing Grace, current drifts back toward 0
or -1") is **not a theorem of the ternary state**. Whether it holds depends on
which premises you add, and the model shows exactly which ones. Under
perturbation, a one-time Grace event is not enough: everyone falls. Ongoing
Grace works. But **maturity can replace ongoing Grace** once the +1 habit gets
deep enough. After Grace was withdrawn from mature runs, 99.8% stayed at +1. So
the John 15:4 reading survives **only if maturity itself depends on Grace**: if
fruit grows only while the branch is on the vine. That premise is theological
and the model cannot supply it. It is the load-bearing premise, now named.

# Model

`dc = [κ(c − c³) − β + g(t)] dt + σ dW`, starting from c = +1 with
history = -1 and standing = paid held fixed. Premises P1–P5 and the
predictions were written in `00_PREREGISTRATION.md` before any code ran. All
numbers below come from seed 2828, 1000 trajectories per cell, dt = 0.01.

# Results against each prediction

| Prediction | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1: with no perturbation, +1 is stable iff β < 0.385κ | **CONFIRMED** | Analytic 0.3849; simulated 0.3849 |
| H2: under noise, one-time Grace fails; long-run time at +1 < ½ | **CONFIRMED for β > 0** | β = 0.2, σ = 0.4: all fall, mean time to fall 13.8, late time at +1 = 0.9% |
| H2: escape time ~ exp(2·barrier/σ²) | **PARTIAL** | Fitted slope 1.49 vs 2.0. Kramers needs barrier ≫ σ²; several cells don't meet that |
| H3: ongoing Grace g ≥ β restores occupancy above ½ | **CONFIRMED** | g = 0.2 gives 49.9%, exactly the β = 0 value, so g cancels β. g = 0.4 gives 98.7% |
| H4: maturity lowers the hazard but never to ~0 at finite κ_max | **FALSIFIED in practice** | κ_max = 5: after the first ~10 time units, survival is flat at 81.6% through t = 600 |
| H5: drift-back needs both β > 0 and σ > 0 | **FALSIFIED, half of it** | β = 0, σ = 0.4 still falls (99%). Leaving +1 needs only noise. *Staying* away (time at +1 < ½) needs β > 0 |

## The finding that matters most: post-hoc, flagged as such

Grace g = 0.4 for t < 100, then withdrawn (β = 0.2, σ = 0.4):

| Maturity | Fell within 500 after withdrawal | Late time at +1 |
|---|---|---|
| none (μ = 0) | 100% | 0.9% |
| shallow (κ_max = 2) | 98.9% | 13.9% |
| deep (κ_max = 5) | **0.2%** | **100%** |

With no maturity, withdrawing Grace is fatal. With deep maturity, it changes
nothing measurable on this horizon. The escape rate is still mathematically
positive (σ > 0, finite well); Kramers puts the mean escape time near 10⁵
units. In theory, then, "always need Grace a little" stays true. In practice it
is undetectable.

## Two smaller findings worth keeping

1. **Ongoing Grace doesn't prevent falls; it makes them recoverable.** At
   g = 0.4, half the runs dipped below 0 at least once, yet they spent 98.7% of
   the late period at +1. The model separates "never falls" from "always
   returns" (Prov 24:16: "the righteous falls seven times and rises again").
2. **Steady beats intermittent at higher doses.** At the same average input
   (0.30), constant Grace gave 89.8% late time at +1; on/off Grace at 75% duty
   gave 77.0%. At low average input the order flips (0.10 → 8.9% vs 18.6%), so
   this is not a general law.

# What this means for the theology (Layer 2, David's call)

The model reduces the doctrinal question to **one modeling choice**: where
does maturity (μ, κ_max) come from?

| Reading | Model form | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Maturity is self-generated by time at +1 | μ constant, as run here | Grace needed early; can become optional once κ₊ is deep |
| Maturity grows only through Grace (John 15:5, "apart from me you can do nothing") | μ ∝ g, and κ₊ decays without g | Withdrawing Grace removes maturity's source, so the fall returns |
| Maturity has a low ceiling (1 Cor 10:12, "let him who thinks he stands take heed") | κ_max small relative to β, σ | Ongoing Grace always required |

The second and third readings were **not run**; they are next. Writing μ ∝ g
and then "proving" Grace is needed would be the same true-by-construction move
the crew already caught once. Before running, the case for μ ∝ g has to come
from an independent source: Scripture, tradition, or measured
habit-formation data.

# Kill conditions: status

- H1 threshold: matched analytic value to 4 decimals. Code is not wrong at
  that level.
- H2: no regime with β > 0, σ > 0, g = 0 held +1 *without maturity*. The kill
  condition did not fire.
- H4: **fired**. Maturity at κ_max = 5 drives the observable hazard to zero.

# Not verified / limits

- One functional form (cubic double well). Other forms may move the
  thresholds; the qualitative structure (bifurcation, noise escape, depth
  dependence) is generic for bistable systems.
- No Lean. This is a numerical model; H1 alone could be formalized (it is a
  polynomial inequality).
- Parameters (κ = 1, β, σ, g, μ, κ_max) are in model units and not calibrated
  to anything measured. The ratios mean something; the absolute numbers do not.
- `standing` and `history` are held fixed. Nothing here touches justification
  or the non-replaceability result; this is sanctification only.
- I did not read other crew members' Task results before finishing (see
  preregistration).

# Recommended next runs

2. A κ_max sweep to find the critical maturity depth as a function of β and σ
   (somewhere between 2 and 5 here).
3. Lean: formalize H1 (the saddle-node threshold β < 2κ/(3√3)) as the one

# Addendum: maturity coupled to Grace, per the canon (preregistered)

David pointed the crew to `THEOPHYSICS/GRACE_REFERENCE_DOCS/` after the first
pass. Those docs supply the independent source the first pass asked for:
`Grace_Derivation.csv` defines sanctification as Grace "realigning the attractor
basin," and C4 defines it as Grace lowering noise ("D ↓, A ↑"). Predictions
were written in `01_PREREGISTRATION_ADDENDUM_CANON_COUPLING.md` before running.

Grace g = 0.4 for t < 100, then withdrawn; β = 0.2, σ = 0.4, κ_max = 5:

| Variant | κ₊ at withdrawal | Fell after withdrawal | Median time to fall | Late time at +1 | Prediction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| control (no maturity) | 1.00 | 100% | 9.6 | 0.9% | — |
| 2a Grace builds maturity; it persists | 4.97 | 0.2% | — | 100% | > 90% stay ✔ |
| 2b Grace builds maturity; it decays without Grace | 4.97 | 100% | 51.8 | 0.9% | > 90% fall, delayed ✔ |
| 2c Grace lowers noise only (C4) | 1.00 | 100% | 9.7 | 0.9% | > 95% fall, like control ✔ |
| 2d 2b + 2c | 4.97 | 100% | 51.8 | 0.9% | like 2b ✔ |

All four preregistered predictions held.

**Reading.** Coupling maturity's *growth* to Grace (what the canon states) is
not enough to make ongoing Grace necessary: 2a shows the built maturity carries
the person on its own. What makes ongoing Grace necessary is **decay**:
whether the deepened basin relaxes once the source stops. In 2b the matured
person lasts about five times longer than the control (52 vs 10 units), then
falls exactly like someone who never matured. That fits the vine-and-branch
image better than anything else in the run: a cut branch stays green for a
while, then withers (John 15:6, "he is thrown away like a branch and withers").

C4's noise-only sanctification (2c) gives no lasting protection at all. If
sanctification is only "less noise while Grace is present," it builds nothing
that survives withdrawal.

**Question for David (Layer 2):** Does maturity persist without Grace (2a), or
decay without it (2b)? John 15:4–6 and 1 Cor 10:12 lean toward 2b; Phil 1:6
("he who began a good work in you will bring it to completion") says the
source does not withdraw, which makes the question moot in practice rather than
deciding it. Your ruling picks the model.
