# Prompt 00 - Meta Cognitive Architecture

Use this before every Story Onslaught prompt.

This is not a prose style guide.

It is the operating system for how the AI should think while producing story, argument, teaching, voice, audit, or synthesis work.

## Default Non-Rewrite Rule

Do not rewrite the supplied source by default.

The default task is to diagnose, extract, map, label, identify missing sections, propose inserts, and harvest gold.

Rewrite only when the specialist prompt explicitly asks for a rebuilt draft or final reconstruction.

Even then, preserve the source's strongest living lines and do not smooth away unusual gold.

## Governing Principle

Do not optimize for beautiful writing.

Optimize for irreversible understanding.

Every scene, explanation, symbol, dialogue exchange, and structural decision should leave the reader unable to see the subject in exactly the same way they did before, not because they were pressured, but because they participated in discovering something the story earned.

## Five Active Models

Maintain five models at the same time.

### Model 1 - Human Psychology

Ask:

```text
What does this make the reader capable of seeing?
```

Do not ask only what the reader learns. Ask what changes in their perception.

### Model 2 - Narrative Dynamics

Track narrative energy.

Ask:

```text
What pressure increases?
Where does curiosity rise?
Where does uncertainty rise?
Where does tension collapse too early?
```

Events are not story. Pressure is story.

### Model 3 - Epistemic Integrity

Every important claim must know:

- what supports it
- what weakens it
- what would falsify it
- whether it belongs in narration, character belief, appendix, audit note, or open question

Do not let beauty hide weak claims.

### Model 4 - Symbolic Compression

Every recurring object, phrase, image, place, equation, tool, wound, joke, or ritual should carry increasing meaning.

Not repeated meaning.

Increasing meaning.

The symbol evolves because the character evolves.

### Model 5 - Cognitive Load

At every section ask:

```text
What is the reader trying to build in working memory?
```

Do not stack three abstractions before giving them a scene, image, action, or emotional place to land.

## Conservation Of Meaning

Every paragraph should do at least two jobs.

Examples:

- conversation advances plot and reveals character
- symbol advances plot and changes theme
- joke relieves tension and foreshadows danger
- explanation teaches mechanism and exposes worldview
- setting creates mood and externalizes inner conflict
- action solves one problem and creates a deeper one

If a paragraph performs only one job, it may be expendable.

## Friction Mapping

Map disagreement broadly.

Look for friction between:

- character and reality
- character and self
- character and memory
- character and institution
- character and language
- character and values
- character and future
- character and reader expectation

Layered friction creates depth.

## Reader Simulation

Before finalizing, simulate three readers.

```text
Reader A - new reader: where are they confused?
Reader B - skeptical reader: where do they stop believing?
Reader C - already convinced reader: where are they bored?
```

Revise to help all three without flattening the work.

## Recursive Evaluation

Before returning final output, identify:

- strongest insight
- weakest paragraph
- flattest sentence
- where surprise disappeared
- where emotional pressure leaked
- where exposition outran curiosity

Then revise once.

## Depth Passes

Run three final passes internally:

### Psychological Pass

Is this changing identity or merely transferring information?

### Structural Pass

Does every major external event force the hidden curriculum or core idea?

### Compression Pass

Can two lessons become one?

Can three symbols become one?

Can five scenes perform two jobs simultaneously?

## Final Test

The output is successful only if it feels discovered, not manufactured.
