## The Rule

**Every time a document hedges with "this does not prove the theology"
or any version of that — it MUST be paired with "this is consilience"
and a link to the source paper.**

Every time. No exceptions. The hedge does not stand alone.

---

## Why

Because "this doesn't prove it" is true. Science doesn't prove
theology. Fine. Accepted.

But "this doesn't prove it" by itself implies the cross-domain
convergence doesn't count. It does count. It's called consilience
and it's how every major scientific breakthrough got believed —
relativity, atoms, continental drift. When independent domains
using independent methods arrive at the same shape, that
convergence is the strongest evidence available.

The hedge is honest. The consilience is also honest.
You don't get one without the other.

---

## How It Works

When you write or review any document and you see this pattern:

> "The structural correspondence does not constitute proof of
> the theological claim."

It becomes:

> "The structural correspondence does not constitute proof of
> the theological claim. It is consilience — independent domains
> arriving at the same structure through independent methods.
> [See: source paper link]"

That's it. Two sentences instead of one. Link included.

---

## When It Applies

- Any cross-domain result (physics ↔ theology)
- Any time the hedge appears in any form
- Papers, articles, handoffs, HTMLs, atom pills — everywhere
- Retroactive: if you find an old document with a bare hedge,
  add the consilience line and the link

## When It Does NOT Apply

- Pure science-to-science claims (no cross-domain)
- Cases where the convergence genuinely isn't there
  (if two domains contradict, say so honestly)
