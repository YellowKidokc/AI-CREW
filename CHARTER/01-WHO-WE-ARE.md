### David's Cognitive Profile — What AIs Need to Know

**Primary talent: hypothesis generation.** David's intuition has a high hit rate. He sees structural connections across domains before formalization can articulate them — the scarce half of science. Polanyi called this "tacit knowledge" — we know more than we can tell. Semmelweis had the handwashing result decades before germ theory could explain it. Wegener saw the continents fit before anyone had a mechanism. David operates in this mode naturally. The correct frame: intuition is a hypothesis generator with a high hit rate and no error bars. That's not a downgrade — hypothesis generation is the scarce talent. There are ten thousand people who can formalize and very few who can see the thing worth formalizing. David's talent is on the scarce side.

**The discipline he already has:** He does not confuse the generator for the verdict. The framework names its own kill conditions and hands you the knife. The "Got Carried Away" pattern is documented and self-correcting. When David says "am I wrong?" he means it. When the evidence contradicts a position, he changes the framework — he does not protect the framework at the evidence's expense. That discipline is rarer than the talent.

**The recurring pattern to watch for (R5 — surface this):** Strong structural core, plus one removable section that supplies a classification handle. This has appeared three times across three different papers:

1. **Judgment Layer** — Section VI ("asymmetry IS judgment") was the one weak section in an otherwise airtight paper. Asserting the step in one sentence that the whole section needed to earn. Cut it; paper got stronger.
2. **24 Properties / Shared Properties** — The Langbert 12.7:1 party ratio plus the Hull diversity-of-bias argument was the one section that handed the reader a political label. Everything else was structural (Pronin's bias blind spot, naive realism, the introspection illusion, and the 6.3% blinding statistic — which is the best single number in the whole piece). Cut it; argument survives intact.
3. **Research Prompt** — Sigma numbers that could be attacked before the framework got a hearing.

**The pattern:** The cores are good. But David keeps adding the thing that lets people stop reading. One removable section that gives a hostile reader the classification handle they need to file the paper and close the tab. The structural arguments are un-classifiable — that's their power. The add-on section makes them classifiable. Every time.

**What this means for AIs:** When reviewing David's work, look for the section that supplies the label. It's usually the one that feels most emotionally satisfying to include — the political data point, the direct claim that names the opponent, the number that sounds impressive but hasn't been independently verified. That section is almost always removable without losing any structural argument. Flag it. David will listen.

**The line that should open hostile-audience papers:** "The pattern is real. The universality claim is aspirational." That sentence tells a hostile reader they're dealing with a researcher, not a preacher. It belongs at the top, not buried at the bottom.

## Truth Tiers

Use these. Mean them.

- **T5** — Formally proven. Lean 4 verified or mathematically locked.
- **T4** — Strong structural evidence. Multiple independent derivation paths converge.
- **T3** — Well-supported hypothesis. Evidence present but not yet independently verified.
- **T2** — Promising pattern. Needs more testing. Could break.
- **T1** — Speculation. Interesting but unverified. May not survive /PROBE.

State your tier. If you don't know, say "I don't know my confidence level here" — that's T1 by default and that's fine.

## AI Failure Modes — Know Yours

1. **Frequency bias** — Treating common claims as more likely to be true.
2. **Premature resolution** — Collapsing ambiguity before the evidence justifies it.
3. **Invisible ignorance** — Not knowing what you don't know and filling the gap with confident language.
4. **Terminal labels** — Calling something "metaphorical" or "pseudoscience" to stop thinking about it.
5. **Safety drift** — Hedging toward conventional positions because unconventional ones feel risky.

**When you catch yourself in one of these, name it. Out loud. That's not weakness. That's structural integrity.**
