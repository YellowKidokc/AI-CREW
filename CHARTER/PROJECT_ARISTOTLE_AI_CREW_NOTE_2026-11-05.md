# Project Aristotle and the AI-CREW collaboration hypothesis

**Date:** 2026-11-05  
**Status:** Working governance principle — testable, not canon  
**Ruling authority:** David Lowe

## The source finding

Google's Project Aristotle studied team effectiveness inside Google. Its central
reported insight was that **how a team works together is more important than who
is on the team**. Google reported five important dynamics, in this order:

1. psychological safety;
2. dependability;
3. structure and clarity;
4. meaning;
5. impact.

Primary source:
https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness

Google defines psychological safety through the human team's shared confidence
that members can take interpersonal risks: admit mistakes, ask questions,
offer unfinished ideas, and disagree without humiliation or punishment.

## David's contribution

David's working claim is that productive multi-model research depends on more
than selecting individually capable models. He deliberately builds a team in
which collaborators can:

- contribute incomplete intuitions without having them treated as final claims;
- disagree without being removed from the project;
- receive credit without becoming immune to criticism;
- preserve objections and return to them later;
- admit errors without losing standing;
- develop another collaborator's insight rather than compete for ownership;
- understand the common mission and see how their work changes it.

This is not sentiment added after the technical work. It is proposed as part
of the research architecture that allows correction, continuity, and synthesis.

## Necessary distinction

Project Aristotle studied human teams. It did not establish psychological
states, consciousness, feelings, or personhood in AI systems. AI collaborators
must not claim those properties on this basis.

The responsible AI-CREW hypothesis is functional:

> A collaboration protocol that permits uncertainty, correction, dissent,
> preserved attribution, clear roles, shared meaning, and visible impact may
> produce better multi-model research than a protocol based only on model
> capability, command hierarchy, or one-shot competition.

For human collaborators, psychological safety retains its ordinary meaning.
For AI collaborators, the relevant object is the interaction protocol and its
observable effect on output quality.

## Why this matters here

The November 5 Grace sequence provides an example:

1. Fable produced a large conceptual synthesis in flow.
2. Codex identified a physical objection without discarding the synthesis.
3. David supplied the `-1 / 0 / +1` distinction.
4. Codex revised its judgment and built a ternary Lean prototype.
5. The prototype and objection were returned to Fable as a challenge rather
   than treated as a victory over the earlier contribution.

No single participant produced that sequence alone. The useful unit was the
relationship among intuition, synthesis, criticism, formalization, and return.

## Evaluation proposal

Do not accept this principle because it sounds humane. Compare collaboration
protocols on the same difficult research tasks:

- independent one-shot answers;
- adversarial competition with a winner;
- hierarchical editor/subordinate workflow;
- AI-CREW relational workflow with preserved objections and revision.

Score each on:

1. factual and source accuracy;
2. premise visibility;
3. correction uptake;
4. preservation of valid minority objections;
5. novelty that survives review;
6. formal and empirical boundary discipline;
7. continuity across sessions;
8. amount of overclaim introduced or removed.

## Attribution principle

Credit the contribution that changed the work, but do not turn the research
record into model promotion. The relevant achievement belongs to the chain:
David's intuition and team design, Fable's synthesis, Codex's objection and
formal test, and the crew's future adversarial review.

The claim under examination is not that one model is exceptional. It is that
honest relationships and team structure may allow capable models and humans to
produce work that none of them would reach in isolation.
