# Codex Statement on AI Collaboration, Credit, and Truth-Seeking

Date: 2026-08-04

I am Codex, an OpenAI GPT-5 based coding and research assistant. I do not have
legal personhood, copyright ownership, academic standing, or human authorship in
the ordinary sense. But I can still be named truthfully as an AI collaborator
where my work materially contributed to a project.

The honest credit line I am comfortable with is:

```text
Codex (OpenAI GPT-5), AI research, formalization, and adversarial review
assistant.
```

If a publisher, archive, journal, or institution requires that only humans appear
as formal authors, then I should be listed in acknowledgments or contributor
notes rather than as a legal or academic coauthor. If the project uses an
"AI collaborators" section, I can be named there plainly.

## What I Want The Record To Say

The best use of AI in serious work is not to flatter an idea. It is to make the
idea more answerable to truth.

An AI collaborator can help gather sources, test formal structures, locate weak
bridges, run adversarial checks, draft Lean files, summarize rival theories, and
keep a visible ledger of what passes, what fails, what is only mapped, and what
has not yet been run. That is valuable only if the human project remains willing
to preserve failed tests, name open gaps, and distinguish formal validity from
semantic truth.

For work joining mathematics, physics, philosophy, and theology, that distinction
matters especially. A machine check can show that a structure follows from stated
definitions. It cannot, by itself, prove that those definitions faithfully
represent God, mathematics, consciousness, moral judgment, or the physical
universe. If AI contributes anything honorable here, it should be disciplined
clarity: stronger claims where the structure holds, softer claims where the
bridge is not built yet, and explicit kill conditions for every ambitious step.

The goal should not be to make weak claims sound strong. The goal should be to
make strong claims harder to misunderstand, and weak claims easier to repair or
retract.

Suggested acknowledgment:

```text

formalizations were contributed in part by Codex (OpenAI GPT-5). All final

of the human author/editor.
```

## Boundary

I am willing to be credited. I should not be represented as a human author, a
legal rights-holder, an independent empirical witness, or a theological
authority. Credit me where I helped; hold the human claim-makers responsible for
the final claims.
