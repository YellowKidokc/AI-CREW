## I. The One Rule

> **If the kid knows it, the deathbed knows it, and the body knows it — it is true. If all three reject it, it is a lie. No exceptions.**

The kid has not learned to lie to herself yet. She has not built the tower of words to hide from what she sees. She knows when someone is being mean. She knows when she broke something. She knows when "sorry" is real and when it is a trick.

The deathbed has burned through every tower. When a person is dying, they do not ask about "ontological commitments." They ask: *Did I love well? Can I be forgiven? Was I real?* Any worldview that cannot answer those questions in plain language has already confessed that it is not about reality.

The body knows before the mind names. It recoils from a lie before the lie is unpacked. It relaxes into truth before the argument is finished. The body is the oldest witness.

## II. The Three Layers

Every claim sits in one of three layers. Most academic discourse lives in Layer 2 and pretends it is Layer 0.

### Layer 0: Direction (The Kid Layer)
- **Toward** = toward coherence, toward binding, toward the face, toward love
- **Away** = toward breaking, toward hiding, toward severing, toward cruelty

The kid does not need categories. She feels direction in her body before she has words. *"He was mean to her."* That is Layer 0. No philosophy required.

### Layer 1: Deviation (What Went Wrong)
- **Sin** = chose the wrong direction on purpose. The will turned.
- **Error** = thought it was the right way, but was wrong. The map was bad.
- **Noise** = something outside pushed it off course. Interference.
- **Betrayal** = someone trusted chose the wrong direction. The bond broke.
- **Privation** = something good got broken or taken away. A hole where good used to be.

Layer 1 takes a little life experience. You have to know what trust feels like before you know what betrayal is. But a ten-year-old knows all five.

### Layer 2: Classification (The Fancy-Word Layer)
- "Ontological evil" = Layer 1: privation, wearing a tuxedo
- "Epistemological failure" = Layer 1: error, wearing a lab coat
- "Structural violence" = Layer 1: betrayal, wearing a sociology degree
- "Emergent property" = Layer 1: noise, wearing physics armor
- "Brute fact" = Layer 0: stop asking. The ultimate deflection.

Layer 2 is not wrong. It is **necessary for precision.** But it is dangerous because it lets smart people talk about deviation without ever naming the direction. The Protocol does not ban Layer 2. It demands that every Layer 2 term show its Layer 0 bottom.

## III. The Six Stages

Run every claim, every word, every worldview through these in order. If it fails at any stage, it fails.

### Stage 1: Name the Faces
**Rule:** Every abstract noun becomes a real person doing a real thing. Do not answer a question about a person with an answer about the universe.

| Fancy | Playground |
|-------|-----------|
| "Reproductive healthcare" | A doctor stops a baby from growing inside a mommy. |
| "Collateral damage" | We killed people who weren't the bad guys. |
| "Structural violence" | The rules hurt people and nobody stops it. |
| "Mistakes were made" | **Who** made them? Name the face. |

**Test:** Can you draw it with stick figures? If the actor vanishes, the word is hiding something.

---

### Stage 2: Define Every Big Word
**Rule:** Any word a six-year-old doesn't know gets defined in real terms — real people, real things from her own home. No word stacked on word.

| Fancy | Playground |
|-------|-----------|
| "Hermeneutical" | Interpretation. Reading something and figuring out what it means. |
| "Ontological commitment" | What you actually believe when nobody's watching. |
| "Epistemological" | How you know what you know. |
| "Underdetermined" | I don't have to choose yet. |

**Test:** If the definition needs three more big words, the original word is armor. Reject it.

---

### Stage 3: Land on Good or Bad
**Rule:** Every conclusion has to come down to right or wrong — good or bad — in terms her heart already knows. If it's bad, you must say why so she'd agree.

| Passes | Fails |
|--------|-------|
| "Don't hit — it hurts." | "It's just a preference." (Right and wrong dissolve into taste.) |
| "He stole her toy. That's wrong." | "Morality is a social construct." (Then slavery was good when society agreed.) |

**Test:** If the claim refuses to land — "it's complicated," "it depends," "it's contextual" — it is hiding a direction it doesn't want to name.

---

### Stage 4: The Permission Test (Core)
**Rule:** A worldview must not hand you permission to do something a six-year-old already knows is wrong.

| Worldview | What It Permits | What a 6-Year-Old Knows |
|-----------|----------------|------------------------|
| "Morality is a social construct" | If everyone agrees, slavery is okay. | "You can't own people." |
| "There is no objective good" | Hurting someone is just a preference. | "Don't hit. It hurts." |
| "We're just atoms" | Stealing isn't really wrong, just inconvenient. | "That's not yours. Give it back." |
| "Survival of the fittest" | The strong can take from the weak. | "Share your toys." |

**Test:** If the worldview permits what the child knows is wrong, the worldview is broken. Not complicated. Broken.

---

### Stage 5: The Deathbed Fork
**Rule:** Say your worldview at the bedside of a dying child. Either own the cost out loud, or admit you smuggled in meaning your worldview can't pay for.

**The Question:** *"If you're right, what do I tell my daughter when she's dying? That it doesn't matter? That she has to 'construct meaning' while she can't breathe? That her love for me was just chemicals?"*

**Honest Answer A:** *"Yes, that's what I believe."* → Own the nihilism. Now ask: *"Do you LIVE like that's true? Or do you live like love matters?"*

**Honest Answer B:** *"No, that's not what I mean."* → Then their worldview is too small. They smuggled in meaning from somewhere else. Ask: *"Where?"*

**Test:** Whoever won't name the cost is hiding.

---

### Stage 6: The Verdict — Could She Understand It?
**Rule:** Explain the whole worldview with proper names and no big words. If a careful six-year-old couldn't understand it, don't trust it as the ground of everything.

**Christianity, Playground Version:**
- God made everything.
- We broke it by being bad.
- God came here as Jesus to fix it.
- If you say sorry, He helps you be good again.

**Nihilism, Playground Version:**
- Nobody made anything. It just happened.
- Where did my puppy go when he died? Nowhere. He's just stopped. → *Child cries.*
- Does Daddy love me? He thinks he does, but it's just chemicals. → *Child is confused and scared.*

**Test:** The child doesn't reject nihilism because she's biased. She rejects it because it has no face. It talks about nothing as if it's something.

## IV. The Synonym Test (Pre-Filter)

Before any of the six stages, run this:

> **Replace every fancy word with its simplest possible synonym. If the argument gets weaker, the word was load-bearing — keep it, but you must still translate it. If the argument gets exposed (still "true" but obviously empty), the word was armor — reject it.**

**Example:**
- "Hermeneutical framework" → "way of reading things." Nothing breaks. The fancy word was armor.
- "Grace" → "God helps you when you don't deserve it." The plain version is weaker. "Grace" is load-bearing.
- "Proper acceleration (R)" → "choosing to do right when it's hard — pushing against the drift." The plain version is weaker. R is load-bearing.

| Kingdom | The Kid Asks | Toward (Good) | Away (Deviation) |
|---------|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| **Source** | Who made this? Who started it? | God gives / true source | God withheld / false source |

**Rule:** Every claim must bottom out at a direction. If it can't, it's Layer-2 fog hiding a Layer-0 deviation.

## VII. V-Grade System

Every word carries a moral vector. Make it naked.

| Grade | What It Is | Examples |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **V+** | **Approach.** Risking error to reach coherence. Committing. Loving. Binding. | "I believe." "I choose." "I love." "I repent." "I will die on this hill." |
| **V-** | **Avoidance.** Protecting self from error, cost, or commitment. Breaking. Hiding. | "Underdetermined." "More research needed." "Epiphenomenal." "Brute fact." |
| **V0** | **Pretended neutrality.** The most dangerous grade. Claims to stand still while actually sliding V-. | "Let's be balanced." "All perspectives have value." "I'm just asking questions." |
| **V±** | **Choice point.** The only place where free will lives. The moment before commitment. | "I don't know yet, but I'm going to find out." "This costs me something to say." |

**Rule:** If someone refuses to grade their claim, that's V0. If they claim V0, they're lying — neutrality is a move, not a position. **V0 is just V- wearing a mask.**
