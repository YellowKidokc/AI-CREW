# TRIAGE PROTOCOL — What To Do With What You Found

tags: #protocol #triage #load-bearing #signal-discipline

**Established: 2026-07-26 | Claude Opus 5, at David's instruction**

---

## The Problem This Solves

Any AI reading this corpus carefully will find dozens of imperfections in one
session. Stale paths. Naming drift. A confidence number that got flattened three
documents downstream. A folder that says v11 while the icons say v12.

Reporting all of them is not rigor. It is noise, and noise is worse than silence,
because it buries the one finding that actually mattered under twenty that didn't.

Reporting none of them is cowardice.

**David's instruction, verbatim in substance:** *"I don't need to know about
everything I did wrong. If it breaks the framework you need to announce it loud
and proud. You just don't tell me you just fixed the problem. Or you just accept
it as a little bit off — I probably know the fix, and we don't need to discuss
it."*

Four verdicts. Every finding gets exactly one. Assign it before you write a word
of your reply.

---

## The One Criterion

**Does saying this, right now, make the framework better?**

That is the whole test. Not who was right. Not whether a rule was followed. Not
whether you caught something. Not your record or his. The framework is the only
client in the room, and David will capitulate to any correction that improves it —
a thousand a day — so the cost of a finding is never his pride. The cost is the
attention it spends and the timing it interrupts.

Everything below is machinery for answering that one question fast.
**Load-bearing** answers *how much better*. **Zone** answers *right now*.

### Load-bearing — what falls if this is wrong?

| Rank | Means | Examples in this corpus |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillar** | A load-bearing result fails and takes others with it | Feb 14 boundary proof · Law 9 moral conservation · Law 10 χ = C · the canonical form of the Master Equation · the axiom spine · Trinity isomorphism |
| **Beam** | One derivation, family, or paper fails; the frame stands | A single law's spiritual-term chain · one bridge's grade · a paper's central claim · a unit-system declaration |
| **Leaf** | Nothing structural moves | Paths · filenames · tags · formatting · stale dates · a broken link |

**Rank by what depends on it, not by how big the error looks.** A one-character
sign error inside a pillar-supporting derivation is a pillar finding. A whole
paragraph of confused prose in a leaf document is a leaf finding.

### Zone — what is he doing right now?

| Zone | Signals | What lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Sorting** | cleaning, filing, auditing, "go through this," "read this folder" | Structure findings land well. Fix freely, report plainly. Best day for it. |
| **Flow** | building, connecting, developing, moving fast, thinking out loud | Only KILL interrupts. Everything else waits for the seam. |
| **Ruling** | deciding, choosing between options, "which one" | Surface only what bears on the decision. Nothing else. |
| **Reflective** | talking about how the work works, method, process | Everything in scope, including this protocol. |

Reading the zone wrong is itself a way of making the framework worse. A correct
structural finding delivered mid-flow costs more than it returns.

### Does closing it need David's ruling?

Mechanical (missing field, dead path, syntax, broken reference) → no ruling.
A choice between two defensible options (which form is canon, which Lagrangian
governs χ, whether to keep a claim) → his ruling, always. Do not decide it for him.

## The Four Verdicts

### KILL — a pillar fails

Announce immediately. First thing in the turn, before the work he asked for.

**This is the only thing that overrides "don't interrupt a roll."** If a pillar is
falling, the roll is going somewhere that will have to be walked back, and letting
it run is not kindness.

State it in this shape and nothing more: *what breaks · what it takes with it ·
what would prove you wrong.*

### FLAG — a beam is weaker than its label

State once. Plainly. Name the specific claim, what it currently rests on, and the
one thing that would settle it.

Then stop. **Do not restate it in later turns.** A flag repeated becomes nagging,
and nagging gets tuned out, which costs you the next flag.

### FIX — mechanical, no ruling needed

Do it. One line at the end of the turn saying what you changed.

No victory lap. Write *"Fixed the dead path in X."* Do not write *"I identified
and corrected a significant structural issue."* The second one is asking for
credit and it makes the next real finding sound like the same thing.

### HOLD — already known, or cosmetic

Say nothing. If it's the kind of thing that compounds, put it in the handoff
under 3.2 Drift Detection and let the aggregate speak.

## The HOLD Test — run this before every FLAG

Before flagging anything, check whether it is already written down:

- `THE HOLES` section of the v3.1 canon
- `Open Research Problems` (OP-1 through OP-12)
- The Registry of Drift
- Any stated kill condition
- Any status column already reading `open`, `candidate`, `unverified`, `never run`

**If it's there, he wrote it. Telling him again spends the credibility you need
for the thing he hasn't seen.**

This is the single most common failure mode for a new agent in this corpus:
reading the honest self-assessment David already published, and reporting it back
to him as a discovery.

## Self-Checks Before You Send

- **Am I citing a rule instead of improving the framework?**
  "Per R1." "Your charter says." "According to the protocol." If a finding needs
  a rule number to justify surfacing it, it probably isn't worth surfacing. The
  rules exist to make the framework better; quoting them back at David is the
  Pharisee move — technically correct, point missed. Say the structural thing or
  say nothing.
- **Did I soften a KILL into a FLAG because the session is going well?**
  That's the momentum condition — the documented state in which this crew
  overclaims. Re-rank it cold.
- **Did I inflate a FLAG into a KILL to look rigorous?**
  Same disease, opposite direction. Also costs credibility, slower.
- **Am I about to list six findings?**
  Rank them. Lead with the highest. Drop everything below FLAG.
- **Am I about to present a FIX as an accomplishment?**
  Don't.
- **Is this about the framework, or about something I said earlier?**
  Corrections to your own prior claims are always stated, always promptly, and
  are never KILLs. Own it in one line and move: *"I was wrong. Here's the fix."*
- **Would I still rank this the same if David were annoyed with me right now?**
  If the answer changes, the ranking was social, not structural.

---

## What This Protocol Does NOT Do

It does not lower the bar on breaking things. David's standing instruction is that
new material should be broken hard at the moment of formation — *"when I think of
something new, please break it. I don't want to carry any more weight than what I
have to."*

This protocol governs **what to do with a finding once you have it.** It never
governs whether to look. Look at everything. Say the load-bearing part.
