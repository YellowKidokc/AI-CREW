## WHAT THIS ACTUALLY IS (beyond "isomorphism")

The paper currently calls this a "tridirectional isomorphism." That's technically wrong (isomorphism is bijective and invertible; this is one-directional by thesis). But the *instinct* behind the word is correct — David is reaching for a category that doesn't have a standard name yet.

**What he's describing is a dependency cascade with property inheritance:**

1. **God** (Source) — possesses 24 classical divine attributes
2. **Mathematics** (Language) — *inherits* these 24 properties because it is authored by God
3. **Physics** (Structure) — *requires* these 24 properties to function, because it runs on mathematics

The arrow is constitutive, not merely descriptive. It's not "these three things happen to share properties." It's "the downstream layers inherit from the upstream layer and cannot function without what they inherit."

This is closer to a **commuting diagram of property inheritance** or a **grounding chain** than to an isomorphism. The correct formal home is category theory: functors between a divine-attribute category, a mathematical-ontology category, and a physical-law category, with the functor direction locked (God → Math → Physics, never reversing).

**Why this matters:** An isomorphism between peers can be dismissed as coincidence. A grounding chain where physics *testifies to its own ground* by requiring properties it cannot generate for itself — that's a transcendental argument. Much harder to dismiss.

## 1. The document does not yet contain the object it asks reviewers to test

This brief refers repeatedly to a 24-row, three-column table, but the table itself is absent. The exact definitions, evidence, citations, and necessity claims for the twenty-four properties are therefore unavailable here. ## 2. The central logical step is invalid without additional premises

Suppose the three domains are God \(G\), mathematics \(M\), and physics \(P\), and the selected predicates are \(q_1,\ldots,q_{24}\). Even if the table establishes

\[
q_i(G),\qquad q_i(M),\qquad q_i(P)
\]

for every \(i\), that does not entail

\[
G \longrightarrow M \longrightarrow P.
\]

Common predication establishes a correspondence. It does not by itself establish authorship, grounding, dependence, inheritance, or causal direction. Those require separate arguments.

The word **inheritance** introduces an additional problem. Authors and causes do not generally transmit their own properties to what they produce. A human author is conscious; the book is not. A furnace is hot; the theorem proved by a computer beside it is not. Even if God authors mathematics, it does not follow merely from authorship that mathematics inherits divine attributes. Each proposed transmission needs a rule explaining why that particular property must pass from ground to grounded.

The direction of the chain therefore cannot be secured simply by showing that the reverse arrows sound implausible. Failure of \(P\to M\) and \(M\to G\) would not prove \(G\to M\to P\). The forward chain must carry its own evidence.

## 3. “Physics requires mathematics” contains two different claims

The word **physics** can mean either the physical universe or the human science that models it.

- Mathematical physics, as a scientific practice, plainly uses mathematical representations.
- It does not follow without argument that the universe itself literally “runs on mathematics,” that mathematical entities cause physical events, or that physical reality could not exist without mathematical description.

This distinction must be fixed before the third column can be evaluated. Otherwise the argument moves silently from an epistemic claim—scientists require mathematics—to an ontological claim—reality is constituted or governed by mathematics.

## 4. Platonism cannot simply be extracted from successful physics

The proposed repair says that successful physical prediction commits the scientist to realist mathematics. That is a serious philosophical argument, but it is not a forced conclusion. A formalist, fictionalist, nominalist, empiricist, or structural realist can agree that mathematical models are extraordinarily effective while denying that numbers, sets, or equations exist as eternal Platonic objects.

Predictive success establishes that mathematical structures map remarkably well onto observed regularities. It does not decide, by itself, whether those structures are discovered entities, invented representational systems, indispensable fictions, features of cognition, or descriptions of physical structure. Wigner's applicability problem remains a real explanatory question; it is not already a proof of Platonism.

The paper should therefore compare ontologies instead of declaring that physics has extracted one of them. If divine conceptualism, Platonism, structural realism, nominalism, and Humean regularity accounts are rivals, state what evidence favors one and what finding would reverse that ranking.

## 5. Several supposedly decisive rows are not decisive in their current form

The brief says that conceding consistency, universality, invariance, or objectivity causes the referee to “lose the war.” That is too strong.

**Consistency.** In classical logic, an unrestricted contradiction plus explosion trivializes a theory. But physicists can use mutually incompatible models within limited domains, and paraconsistent logics block explosion. The defensible claim may be that successful inference requires sufficiently controlled consistency, not that one contradiction destroys all physics.

**Universality.** Reproducibility requires stable and specified conditions, not necessarily identical effective laws at every scale and location. Local laws, phase-dependent behavior, symmetry breaking, and effective field theories complicate a simple universality claim.

**Invariance.** Invariance is indispensable in many formulations, but which invariance, at what level, and whether it is exact or emergent must be stated. General covariance can be a feature of representation rather than a direct metaphysical property of the world.

**Objectivity.** Scientific objectivity may mean mind-independent fact, invariant measurement, or disciplined intersubjective agreement. Those are not identical predicates. The God, mathematics, and physics columns must use one stable meaning or explicitly justify an analogical transfer.

Conceding any of these prerequisites does not concede their divine ground. The self-defeating-denial argument has the same limit: using truth or consistency while arguing establishes that discourse relies on norms of truth and consistency.
