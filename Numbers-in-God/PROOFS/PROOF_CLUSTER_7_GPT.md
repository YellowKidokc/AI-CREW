## 0. Strongest possible reconstruction

These rows are tested in their strongest plausible forms:

- **Simple:** non-composite or minimally generative.
- **Infinite:** lacking an intrinsic finite bound.
- **Perfect:** complete or exactly conforming to its defining standard.

The test does not permit switching between these meanings during a row. If divine simplicity becomes mathematical elegance and then physical compactness, the word remains the same while the predicate changes. That is precisely what the proof standard prohibits.

Domain lock:

- **God:** classical Christian divine nature.
- **Mathematics:** mathematical truths and structures.
- **Physics:** fundamental physical law-structure plus the concrete universe it governs, with the distinction made whenever it matters.

## 1. SIMPLE

### Test 1 — Define it identically

#### Candidate A: absence of metaphysical parts

\[
S_1(x):=x\text{ is not composed of more fundamental constituents.}
\]

- God may satisfy (S_1) under divine simplicity.
- Mathematics as a whole does not: it contains many structures, axioms, relations, and domains.
- Physical reality does not demonstrably satisfy it: physical theories contain multiple fields, symmetries, parameters, and states.

Candidate A fails.

#### Candidate B: minimal generative description

\[
S_2(x):=x\text{ generates its consequences from an irredundant basis.}
\]

- Mathematics and physics can sometimes be compressed into economical axioms or actions.
- God is not an axiom set or description, and divine simplicity is not equivalent to short description length.

Candidate B also fails as an identical predicate.

### Test 2 — Required, not optional

- Divine simplicity is affirmed by many classical traditions but disputed by other orthodox theists; it is not definitional across every Christian model.
- Mathematics does not die if an axiom system is redundant or complex.
- Physics does not die if its fundamental description has multiple fields or constants.

**Ruling:** Not required across the domains.

### Test 3 — Same operation

Divine simplicity blocks composition and dependence. Mathematical simplicity supports economy or independence. Physical simplicity supports compression, unification, or model preference. These are distinct operations.

**Ruling:** Fails.

### Test 4 — Forward reading

A simple rational source might motivate a search for unified, economical laws. It does not deductively entail that the shortest true physical theory will be simple to human minds, nor that reality has no fundamental plurality.

**Forward ruling:** Heuristic expectation, not structural prediction.

### Test 5 — Backward reading

Compact physical equations do not imply a metaphysically simple personal author. They are also expected under unification, symmetry, selection effects, structural realism, or the fact that compressible regularities are what science can model.

**Backward ruling:** Non-unique.

### Test 6 — Kill attempt

Counterexample: a mathematically consistent universe governed by several irreducible fields and constants remains a physical universe. Its complexity would not disprove mathematics or God, which shows that simplicity is not a necessary inherited property of the chain.

**Final verdict — Simple:** **FAIL.** Preserve “generative economy” as a research heuristic, not an identity property.

### Six-year-old version

God not being made of pieces is not the same thing as an equation being short. A giant box of mathematics contains many pieces, and the universe may need several rules. We used one word for three different ideas. That row does not count.

## 2. INFINITE

### Test 1 — Define it identically

Candidate predicate:

\[
I_\infty(x):=\neg\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }n\text{ is an intrinsic upper bound on }x.
\]

- God is traditionally called infinite in power, knowledge, presence, and perfection—not a set with unbounded cardinality.
- Mathematics contains finite and infinite objects and admits several non-equivalent infinities. Mathematics as a practice may be indefinitely extensible.
- Physical reality may be spatially finite or infinite; observable entropy and information can be finite; current physics does not require the concrete universe to be infinite in the same sense as God.

**Ruling:** No identical predicate.

### Test 2 — Required, not optional

- Lack of creaturely limitation is central to classical divine infinity.
- Individual mathematical domains need not be infinite.
- Physics can operate in finite state spaces, bounded regions, finite-duration models, and possibly a spatially finite cosmos.

**Ruling:** Not required across all three.

### Test 3 — Same operation

Divine infinity is qualitative non-limitation. Mathematical infinity is formal cardinal, ordinal, analytic, or extensibility structure. Physical infinity is a model-dependent claim about magnitude, extent, divisibility, or state space. These operations differ.

**Ruling:** Fails.

### Test 4 — Forward reading

An unlimited source does not require every created quantity to be infinite. Finite creation is fully compatible with an infinite creator. Therefore a finite universe is not a failed prediction of theism.

**Forward ruling:** The proposed transmission is expressly selective; no necessity follows.

### Test 5 — Backward reading

Even if physics contained actual infinities, they would not uniquely indicate God. Mathematical Platonism and naturalistic cosmologies contain infinity without a divine author. If physics is finite, the backward path disappears entirely.

**Backward ruling:** Fails uniqueness and necessity.

### Test 6 — Kill attempt

Counterexample: a finite physical universe with finite information capacity can still instantiate exact mathematical relations and be created by God. Because the hypothesis permits both finite and infinite physics, “Infinite” discriminates nothing in the third domain.

**Final verdict — Infinite:** **FAIL.** Retain “unbounded formal extensibility” as a mathematics-specific observation, not a tri-domain inheritance proof.

### Six-year-old version

An artist can be more creative than one painting. The painting does not have to be infinitely big. In the same way, an infinite God could make a finite world. So the world's size cannot prove this row.

## 3. PERFECT

### Test 1 — Define it identically

Candidate predicate:

\[
P_f(x,C):=x\text{ completely satisfies standard }C\text{ with no defect.}
\]

This predicate requires an independently specified criterion (C).

- God is called morally and metaphysically perfect: the standard is identified with or grounded in God's nature.
- A mathematical proof can be correct relative to axioms and inference rules; mathematics as a whole is not “perfect” in the moral sense and can include incomplete theories.
- A physical system can exactly satisfy an idealized model, but actual scientific laws may be effective and approximate. The universe includes disorder, decay, suffering, and broken symmetry.

**Ruling:** “Perfect” changes its criterion between domains. A two-place formal schema is shared; the substantive predicate is not.

### Test 2 — Required, not optional

- Divine perfection is required by the proposed classical God-profile.
- Mathematics requires correctness for valid proofs, not perfection of every formal system.
- Physics requires empirical adequacy and consistency within regimes, not a perfect universe or a final perfect human theory.

**Ruling:** Not required identically.

### Test 3 — Same operation

In theology, perfection is maximal excellence. In mathematics, exactness is binary satisfaction of formal rules. In physics, accuracy is agreement between model and measurement within uncertainty. These are three different evaluation operators.

**Ruling:** Fails identical operation.

### Test 4 — Forward reading

A perfect God may create a good but finite and freely developing world. Theism therefore does not predict physical perfection in the sense of no decay, approximation, or suffering. To claim otherwise creates a direct problem of evil and finitude.

**Forward ruling:** No identical transmitted perfection.

### Test 5 — Backward reading

Exact mathematical prediction can motivate the question “exact relative to what?” It does not identify moral perfection or a personal ground. Structural realism and Platonism can explain mathematical exactness without divine goodness.

**Backward ruling:** Non-unique.

### Test 6 — Kill attempt

Counterexamples include incomplete formal systems, effective physical theories, measurement uncertainty, and lawful physical states containing disorder. Redefining perfection as “whatever the domain happens to be” makes the predicate vacuous.

**Final verdict — Perfect:** **FAIL.** Move exactness/correctness to the Logical or Evaluative cluster, with the governing criterion explicitly typed. Do not retain “Perfect” as a shared identity row.

### Six-year-old version

A perfect person, a perfectly drawn circle, and a thermometer that reads exactly right are called “perfect” for different reasons. We have to say what rule each one is being measured by. The same word does not make them the same property.
