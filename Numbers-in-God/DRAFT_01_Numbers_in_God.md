# Numbers in God

Your uncle is right about one thing.

You cannot put God into mathematics.

A human mind cannot take the infinite God, push Him through a symbolic machine, and make an equation contain Him. That would be too small. It would make God an object inside the system, one more term among terms, one more variable waiting to be solved.

But that was never the real claim.

The real claim is not that we put God into numbers. The real claim is that number, order, truth, intelligibility, and rational structure were never godless things.

Mathematics is strange. Not strange because it is complicated, though it can be. Strange because it seems to stand in more than one world at once. It is not physical in the ordinary sense. The number seven has no weight. A theorem has no location. An equation is not made of the thing it describes. And yet physics, the study of the physical world, cannot move an inch without mathematics.

The most material science we have depends on something that is not material.

That is the pressure.

Not a trick. Not numerology. Not a hidden Bible code. Not a claim that a triangle proves the Trinity or that every prime number is a sermon.

The pressure is simpler and deeper:

Why does reality run on abstract, stable, intelligible order that finite minds can discover?

Every worldview has to answer that.

## The Threefold Problem

The mystery of mathematics is not one problem. It is three problems braided together.

First, mathematics appears to have an abstract reality. Mathematical truths are not invented the way games are invented. We can invent notation. We can invent methods. We can invent names. But we do not make two plus two equal four by social agreement. We discover that it is so. We do not vote pi into irrationality. We find it.

Second, mathematics applies to the physical world with unreasonable power. Physics is not merely decorated with mathematics. It is built with it. Measurement, prediction, symmetry, invariance, conservation, field equations, statistical mechanics, quantum theory, relativity: all of them depend on mathematical structure. If the world were not mathematically intelligible, physics would collapse into description without law.

Third, human minds can know mathematics. Finite, embodied, error-prone creatures can grasp necessary truths. A mind made of dust can know a theorem that is not dusty. A brain changing in time can understand truths that do not change with time.

That triangle is the real argument:

1. Abstract mathematical truth.
2. Physical applicability.
3. Human knowability.

Any explanation that handles only one corner is incomplete.

Platonism can say mathematical objects exist abstractly, but then it owes an account of why those abstract objects govern physical reality and why physical minds can know them. Nominalism can say mathematics is a human construction, but then it owes an account of why the construction fits the deep structure of nature before we arrive. Naturalism can say math is useful modeling, but then it owes an account of why the most successful models reach so far into the hidden architecture of the world.

Christian theism has an answer ready at the root.

The world is intelligible because it is created by Logos. Mathematical truth is not an independent eternal realm standing beside God. It is not a second god made of abstractions. It is also not a fiction produced by human brains. It is a created-world witness to the rational order of the One by whom and through whom all things hold together.

That does not prove Christianity by force.

It makes Christianity a serious explanation of the whole pattern.

## What Mathematics Shows Before Theology Names It

Before the paper speaks theologically, it has to let mathematics be seen plainly.

Mathematics is stable. Once proved, a theorem does not decay. Human understanding can improve or fail, but the truth itself is not weather.

Mathematics is objective. A proof does not become valid because someone prefers it. It either follows or it does not.

Mathematics is rational. It is not mere sequence. It is ordered inference.

Mathematics is abstract. It is not made of chalk marks, ink, pixels, neurons, or sound waves. Those things carry mathematical symbols, but they are not the mathematical truth itself.

Mathematics is generative. From small beginnings, enormous worlds unfold. A few axioms can generate vast structures. A compact equation can govern an ocean of cases.

Mathematics is constraining. It permits and forbids. It says this follows, that does not; this structure is possible, that one collapses.

Mathematics is intelligible. It can be known, taught, checked, repeated, extended, and discovered from different directions by different minds.

These are not decorative properties. These are the reasons mathematics can do the work it does.

Physics depends on them.

If mathematics were inconsistent, derivation would fail. If it were not objective, prediction would become preference. If it were not stable, repeatability would collapse. If it were not abstract enough to outrun any one physical instance, it could not govern whole classes of physical behavior. If it were not intelligible, science could not become knowledge.

This is the hard floor of the argument.

At K0, without theological vocabulary, we can say this much:

Reality is mathematically intelligible, and physics depends on that intelligibility.

That is enough to make the question unavoidable.

## The Christian Answer

Christian theology does not begin with mathematics. It begins with God.

But if God is the Creator, then mathematics is not religiously neutral in the deepest sense. It may be methodologically neutral for doing calculations. A believer and an unbeliever can solve the same equation. But the existence of stable truth, rational order, and intelligible structure is not metaphysically neutral. It belongs somewhere.

The Christian claim is that it belongs in the Logos.

The Gospel of John does not say that the world began with noise. It says, "In the beginning was the Word." Logos is not merely a spoken syllable. It carries reason, order, intelligibility, expression, and meaning. Creation is not brute fact plus later interpretation. Creation is spoken order.

That gives mathematics a home.

Mathematical truths are not God. They are not creatures in the ordinary physical sense. They are not human inventions. They are rational structures grounded in the divine mind and expressed in created order. We know them because we are made as rational creatures inside a rationally authored world.

This is why Poythress matters for this argument. His strongest contribution is not merely saying, "God likes math." It is the three-perspective structure: mathematics has an abstract dimension, a physical-world dimension, and a human-mental dimension. Those three dimensions belong together because God is the source of all three: the truth known, the world structured, and the mind capable of knowing.

That is the bridge.

Not God inserted into arithmetic.

God as the reason arithmetic, physics, and mind can meet.

## The 24 Properties

The 24-properties argument should be handled with discipline.

Some properties are hard requirements for physics and mathematics:

- consistency;
- rationality;
- intelligibility;
- objectivity;
- invariance;
- constraint;
- stability;
- generativity.

Without these, science does not merely become harder. It loses its conditions of possibility.

Other properties are metaphysical readings of mathematics:

- necessity;
- immateriality;
- eternality;
- infinity;
- exactness;
- transcendence over physical instances.

These are serious, but they require philosophy. A mathematical Platonist may accept many of them without becoming a Christian. That means the paper must not pretend the debate is already over at this layer.

Other properties are theological bridge readings:

- law-giving;
- judging;
- order-giving;
- foundational;
- necessary for knowledge;
- unity;
- simplicity.

These may be true and powerful, but they need careful translation. "God judges" and "mathematics distinguishes true from false" are not the same sentence in the same register. They may share a deep structure of standard, evaluation, and verdict, but that structure has to be named rather than assumed.

This does not weaken the 24-property argument.

It saves it.

If every property is forced to carry the same kind of claim, the paper breaks. But if the properties are sorted by claim lane, the pattern becomes stronger:

Hard requirements show that science depends on rational structure.

Metaphysical properties show that mathematics cannot be reduced easily to matter or convention.

Theological bridge properties show why Christian theism has a natural explanatory home for the whole pattern.

The result is not a cheap analogy. But it is also not yet a formal isomorphism.

The honest phrase is structured correspondence.

Formal isomorphism can come later if the domains, maps, relations, and preservation rules are built.

## Why Rival Explanations Are Not Enough

A skeptic has options.

He can say mathematics is a human language. But then he has to explain why the language reaches into physical reality so deeply before humans know what they are looking for.

He can say mathematics is an abstract Platonic realm. But then he has to explain why that realm connects to the physical world and to human minds.

He can say mathematics is just pattern recognition. But then he has to explain why the patterns are stable enough to launch satellites, predict particles, and describe structures no human sense could directly see.

He can say any possible universe would have quantities, ratios, and therefore mathematics. That is a serious objection. But it does not remove the deeper question. It only moves it: why is there a rationally ordered universe at all, and why are rational creatures capable of discovering its order?

The Christian answer is not embarrassed by those questions.

The Christian answer expects them.

If the world is created through Logos, then intelligibility is not surprising. If humans are made in the image of God, then mathematical knowledge is not an accident. If creation is ordered by divine wisdom, then physical law is not brute regularity floating without ground.

Christianity does not need to deny that mathematics can be studied by unbelievers. It says that every true act of mathematical knowing is possible because reality is already held in truth.

The gift can be received while the Giver is denied.

That is not new in Christian theology. That is Romans 1 applied to number, structure, and law.

## What This Paper Must Not Become

This paper must not become numerology.

Biblical number symbolism is real in many places. Seven, twelve, forty, and other numbers often carry theological weight in Scripture. Revelation especially uses numbers symbolically. But symbolism is not the same thing as divination, code hunting, or making arbitrary totals carry secret authority.

The argument here is not "look, a number appeared."

The argument is "look at what number is."

Number is ordered. Number is intelligible. Number is abstract. Number is stable. Number is generative. Number can be known. Number can govern physical description without being physical itself.

That is far deeper than numerology.

This paper must also avoid pretending that mathematics captures God.

Cantor is useful here. His work on transfinite numbers shows that mathematics can speak rigorously about infinities. But even there, Christian theology must keep a boundary. The transfinite is not God. Mathematical infinity is not divine infinity. God is not merely the largest set, the biggest number, or the final object in a hierarchy.

Mathematics can witness infinity.

It cannot contain the Infinite.

That distinction matters.

## The Real Force

The force of this argument is cumulative.

No single property proves the whole case. Consistency alone does not prove Christianity. Infinity alone does not prove the Trinity. The usefulness of equations alone does not prove the Incarnation.

But taken together, the pattern creates pressure.

Mathematics is abstract but applies to matter.

It is stable but known by changing minds.

It is not physical but physics depends on it.

It constrains reality but is not one more object inside reality.

It is discovered by persons and yet not invented by those persons.

It is rational, intelligible, generative, objective, and strangely fitted to the world.

Christian theism does not have to force these facts into its frame. They already fit.

The Logos doctrine says that rational order is not accidental. Creation doctrine says the physical world is not brute chaos. The image of God says finite minds can genuinely know. General revelation says creation can testify without becoming Scripture. Divine simplicity says God's truth, wisdom, goodness, and rationality are not separable parts added together. They are one life.

So when mathematics displays unity, rationality, truth, order, and intelligibility, the Christian should not be surprised.

The universe is not indifferent.

It is legible.

## The Claim

Here is the claim in its guarded form:

Mathematics does not prove God by coercion. It bears witness by structure. Its abstractness, stability, intelligibility, objectivity, necessity, and generative power create a deep explanatory pressure. Physics depends on that structure. Human minds can know that structure. Christian theism gives a unified account of all three by grounding mathematical truth in the Logos and reading the universe as rationally authored creation.

That is the argument.

Not that numbers are God.

Not that God is an equation.

Not that physics proves the gospel.

But that number, truth, order, and intelligibility are not homeless.

They belong to the world because the world belongs to God.
