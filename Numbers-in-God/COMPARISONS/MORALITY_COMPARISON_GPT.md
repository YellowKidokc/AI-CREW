## 0. Question lock

The lane contains four different explananda:

1. **Cooperative behavior:** why organisms help, reciprocate, punish, and form groups.
2. **Moral psychology:** why humans experience approval, guilt, indignation, empathy, and obligation.
3. **Moral truth:** whether any moral proposition is objectively correct.
4. **Moral authority:** why a true moral requirement binds an agent even when violation is advantageous and undetected.

Evolutionary theory directly addresses the first and substantially addresses the second. It does not, without an added metaethical bridge, settle the third or fourth. Theophysics directly proposes an answer to the third and fourth; it requires a secondary developmental and historical account for the first and second. These scopes must not be exchanged during the comparison.

## 1. The Moral Evaluation Operator

Define:

\[
\mathcal{E}_m(a,s,C,A)\mapsto(V,O,\Delta A),
\]

where:

- (a) = act, intention, policy, or character state;
- (s) = relevant circumstances;
- (C) = moral criterion;
- (A) = agent state;
- (V) = verdict: right, wrong, permitted, required, supererogatory, or indeterminate;
- (O) = authority or binding force of the verdict;
- (Delta A) = appropriate response: action, restraint, restitution, praise, blame, repentance, or revision.

Three operators must not be conflated:

| Operator | Criterion | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Evolutionary selection | reproductive consequences in an environment | differential transmission |
| Social enforcement | group norm, law, reputation, power | reward, punishment, inclusion, exclusion |
| Moral evaluation | criterion of rightness/goodness | justified verdict and alleged obligation |

The first two can explain why a moral feeling or norm spreads. They do not automatically establish that the norm is correct. Conversely, the existence of moral truth does not by itself explain how humans acquired their particular moral dispositions.

## 3. Boundary transformation — Theophysics morality lane

### God → objective moral order → finite agents

| Field | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Preserved** | goodness as non-arbitrary; personal worth; rational evaluability; relational orientation; distinction between right and wrong |
| **Lost / not transmitted** | omniscience; moral perfection; infallible judgment; aseity; unlimited power to realize the good |
| **Introduced** | finite perspective; uncertainty; developmental learning; conflict among goods; temptation; social dependence; responsibility under limited knowledge |
| **Forbidden** | an arbitrary command becoming good solely through power [forbidden by the proposed nature-grounded model]; a finite person's preference constituting universal goodness merely by being preferred [type error] |

### Transfer rule

\[
\text{perfect personal goodness}
\rightarrow
\text{creation for loving relationship}
\rightarrow
\text{agents capable of reasons and response}
\rightarrow
\text{finite moral knowledge and free action}.
\]

This rule makes moral likeness and moral limitation jointly expected. It still requires an account of how created agents reliably recognize genuine duties.

## 4. Boundary transformation — evolutionary morality lane

### Evolutionary dynamics → moral psychology and social norms

| Field | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Preserved** | behavior-affecting dispositions that remain reproductively or culturally transmissible; sensitivities to care, reciprocity, reputation, hierarchy, coalition, and punishment |
| **Lost / not transmitted** | variants eliminated by drift, selection, institutional suppression, or cultural replacement; no guarantee that truth-neutral or costly dispositions persist |
| **Introduced** | reflective moral concepts; explicit rules; legal institutions; universalization; philosophical criticism; novel cultural norms built from evolved capacities |
| **Forbidden** | deriving “objectively right” from “selected,” “common,” or “socially enforced” without a separate bridge [logical non sequitur]; claiming evolution disproves objective morality [scope error] |

Evolution does not forbid objective morality. It leaves moral ontology underdetermined while explaining much of the causal route by which human beings came to make and enforce moral judgments.

## 5. Direct answer: Does evolution explain obligation?

### It explains three important things

1. why organisms capable of cooperation can outcompete or outlast pure defectors under specified conditions;
2. why humans possess emotions and expectations that support reciprocal and group life;
3. why norm violation can feel urgent, shameful, blameworthy, or dangerous.

### It does not establish one further proposition

\[
\text{This act is objectively required even if every organism benefits from denying it.}
\]

To obtain that conclusion, a naturalist must add and defend a metaethical bridge. That is possible in principle; it is not supplied by evolutionary theory itself.

Theophysics supplies a candidate bridge by locating moral value in the nature of a necessarily good personal ground. Its remaining burden is to show:

1. that objective obligation exists;
2. that goodness is coherently identical with or grounded in God's nature;
3. that this relation produces obligations for created agents;
4. that humans have a reliable but fallible route to knowing those obligations;
5. that secular moral realism or constructivism cannot do the same work more economically.

## 6. Clean verdict

| Question | Winner / ruling |
|---|---|
| Historical mechanism for cooperation | Evolutionary and cultural explanation |
| Development of moral emotions | Evolutionary-developmental account |
| Explanation of norm variation and bias | Evolutionary-cultural account |
| Objective truth of moral propositions | Not settled by evolution |
| Categorical binding authority | Theophysics offers a direct candidate ground; proof remains comparative |
| Complete account | Theistic evolution can combine the strongest non-competing layers: God grounds value and obligation; evolution describes development and transmission |

The strongest conclusion is not “evolution failed.” It is:

> Evolution explains how cooperative dispositions and moral psychology can arise and spread. It does not, without an additional metaethical premise, convert adaptive success into objective obligation. Theophysics addresses that missing normative layer, but must still defeat secular moral realism and explain moral knowledge.

## 7. Six-year-old version

Animals and people learn that helping, sharing, and keeping promises can make a group work better. Evolution can help explain why we have those feelings and habits.

But “this helped our group” is not always the same as “this was right.” A whole group can benefit from hurting somebody outside the group. To decide whether that is truly wrong, we need a rule deeper than which group survived.

The God explanation says the deeper rule comes from a perfectly good Person who made people valuable. That explains where a real “ought” could come from. We still have to prove that this God is the source and that we understand the rule correctly.
