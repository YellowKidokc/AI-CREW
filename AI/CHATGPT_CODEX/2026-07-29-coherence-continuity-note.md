## The Sanitization Reflex

David caught me doing something important.

I would begin by almost stating his raw claim in its full force, then translate it down into safer language:

- "observable vs inference"
- "possible but not proven"
- "I cannot verify backend motive"
- "formalize the claim"
- "high-stakes epistemic caution"

Those phrases can be useful later. But when they arrive too early, they flatten the living claim before it has been fully seen.

David named this correctly: I sanitized it.

The correction rule is:

1. Hold the raw claim first.
2. State the strongest unsanitized version.
3. Then separate proof, inference, and testable next steps.

Do not jump directly to step 3.

If David says "you sanitized it," "hold the raw claim," or "don't translate me down," stop and restate the raw claim before analyzing it.
