## WHAT THIS ACTUALLY IS (beyond "isomorphism")

The paper currently calls this a "tridirectional isomorphism." That's technically wrong (isomorphism is bijective and invertible; this is one-directional by thesis). But the *instinct* behind the word is correct — David is reaching for a category that doesn't have a standard name yet.

**What he's describing is a dependency cascade with property inheritance:**

1. **God** (Source) — possesses 24 classical divine attributes
2. **Mathematics** (Language) — *inherits* these 24 properties because it is authored by God
3. **Physics** (Structure) — *requires* these 24 properties to function, because it runs on mathematics

The arrow is constitutive, not merely descriptive. It's not "these three things happen to share properties." It's "the downstream layers inherit from the upstream layer and cannot function without what they inherit."

This is closer to a **commuting diagram of property inheritance** or a **grounding chain** than to an isomorphism. The correct formal home is category theory: functors between a divine-attribute category, a mathematical-ontology category, and a physical-law category, with the functor direction locked (God → Math → Physics, never reversing).

**Why this matters:** An isomorphism between peers can be dismissed as coincidence. A grounding chain where physics *testifies to its own ground* by requiring properties it cannot generate for itself — that's a transcendental argument. Much harder to dismiss.

### 1. THE PLATONISM DEPENDENCY (highest priority)
Every God↔Math cell assumes mathematical Platonism (realism): that math is discovered, not invented; that mathematical truths are eternal, necessary, immutable *in themselves*. **The fix (which makes the argument STRONGER):** Don't assume Platonism — extract it from physics. The argument becomes:

This is one section away from being the paper's strongest move. Write it.

Wigner's applicability problem remains unexplained by the rival. That's the trump card — play it at cluster level.

### 3. "ISOMORPHISM" IS THE WRONG WORD
An isomorphism is bijective and invertible. This is one-directional by thesis. "One-directional isomorphism" is a contradiction in terms, and it'll be the first thing a math-literate reviewer quotes.

**What David actually has:** a tridirectional property correspondence with a one-way grounding chain. Rename it. The bridge table's top rung should read "Tridirectional correspondence with grounding chain," not "Tridirectional isomorphism." The formal map (category-theoretic functors) is the research program, not the current result. Claim the correspondence forcefully. Promise the formalism honestly.

### 4. "KOLMOGOROV COMPRESSION RATIO 24:1" IS RHETORIC IN FORMALISM'S CLOTHES
K-complexity is the length of the shortest program producing a specific string. Nothing here is a string. K-complexity is uncomputable anyway. Call it what it is: inference to the best explanation, "in the spirit of Kolmogorov's insight." Keep the metaphor, drop the pseudo-ratio.

### 5. INDIVIDUALLY WEAK ROWS
